# Introduction
Let's take on college. Let's see how this journey transforms, because again, I'm deeply conflicted about college. I think this applies specifically to my situation more than as a general rule, but I think it helps to put my thoughts out there and let them swirl. Big communities are there to see people agree or disagree, right?

# The Problem with College Expectations
The fundamental problem I have with college is that, from a younger age, I was promised a lot of value. The thing about CS is that most of it is available online today. It's very easy to come across situations where individuals are getting picked up by bigger companies because they picked up most of the skills online. Software is so diverse and there's a lot of development happening worldwide that's easy to document, and it's easy to pick it up from there as someone just on the Internet.

You can't necessarily say the same for a lot of other domains, especially natural sciences, where your opportunities and the knowledge you can gain are limited to the research and intellect that your college is able to offer in terms of the professors they hire. Coming from a background where college itself was really valued as an experience, it seemed mandatory to get into a good college if you wanted to get a good life after that. I have to say I'm pretty disillusioned by that right now.

# The Reality of CS Education
A lot of the stuff that I learned through my course is very critical and not very practical, and my college in particular doesn't seem to value exploration into utilising your technical talents outside of the curriculum. Maybe that's just because of how the education system is structured (maybe it's just "focus on your degree and everything will be set after that"), but most of what you need to get a job in CS or CS-related fields is not something that you can pick up in a lecture theatre.

You would be starved for the resources to find jobs outside of your campus, and I don't know why, but I think I just expected more from an institution that I'm selling out a quarter mil for to help me provide the resources I need to achieve my ambitions, not just proceed on some route that all my other predecessors and seniors have gone through.

# The Value of a Good University
I will say this though: the value of a good university, I believe, comes from the calibre of people that they let in. What do I mean by that? Well, most top universities (Stanford, Harvard, Cambridge, Oxford, Imperial, etc.) tend to bring in very inquisitive individuals. The selection process is tailored to bring in some of the brighter minds, and so you find yourself connecting with very diverse people from various backgrounds who are very smart. You can always afford to learn something from each of them regardless of their background. Furthermore, they tend to bring in expert researchers at the top of their fields, cutting-edge. You'd have an advantage to learn something new, but how much of it plays into achieving your dreams for those of us who aren't getting into academia and research.

I think that provides a good platform. College as a whole provides you a good platform to network well. I hate saying it, but I don't mean the cheap kind of connecting on social media or just commenting congratulations every now and then. I mean legitimate, deep connections with people who share similar interests with you. I think college's real value stems from that, not the time you spend in a lecture, because honestly, most of the material for my course I found outside of it as well.

# Beyond the Curriculum: Real Value
If anything, it's just using a sunk cost fallacy mindset to force you to pick things up from the lectures, but the real value comes from the connections you're able to make, the extra resources you have access to in terms of labs, and just the brand associated with that institution.

There's a lot more value in the entire college experience that I took for granted initially, but I've come around to realise that that's where the bulk of the experience lies for me. With the deadlines and the work that they give us, it's impossible for me to now shudder at the workload. I've gotten over the fear of how much work there is to do, and I guess the pressure they put on us in such institutions, at least as an engineering student, allows you to adopt a framework that helps you take on larger tasks, break them down into fundamentals, and address them one by one without worrying about the outcomes necessarily.

Conclusion
Let's see how this journey transforms, because again, I'm still figuring it out. I'm keen to see how this experience as a whole eventually transforms me.
